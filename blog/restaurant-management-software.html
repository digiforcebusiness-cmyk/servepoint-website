Restaurant management software vs. a POS system

A POS focuses on orders and checkout. Restaurant management software is a broader category that can also include inventory, reporting, staff scheduling, accounting or reservations. A POS with built-in kitchen and stock tools can cover much of an independent restaurant's daily service, but it does not automatically replace every back-office tool.

Back-office and restaurant operations software

Front-of-house tools handle what guests see: orders, bills and receipts. Back-office or restaurant operations software handles the work behind them, such as stock counts, sales reporting, scheduling, purchasing and accounting. Some restaurant management systems cover both, while others connect separate tools.

Small teams rarely need every module on day one. Cover orders, kitchen communication, stock and daily reporting first. Add a specialist tool when a real problem justifies the extra cost and training.

How do you choose between restaurant management platforms?

Comparison pages all start to look alike, so judge each platform against your own service instead of the longest feature list.

  1. Describe your busiest hour. Write down who takes orders, who prepares them, and where mistakes or delays happen today.
  2. Separate needs from extras. Mark each feature as required on day one, useful later or unnecessary.
  3. Price your real setup. Ask for the total with your number of devices, kitchen screens and locations.
  4. Test it with the people who will use it. Ask a server and a cook to run real orders on your own devices.
  5. Check the way out. Confirm the contract length and how you can export your data if you leave.

Will servers and kitchen staff actually use it?

Software only helps if the team uses it in the middle of a rush. During your test, time how long a modified order takes, count the taps for the most common items and ask the people on shift what slowed them down. A plainer system your staff enjoys using often beats a longer feature list they work around.

Restaurant management app or desktop system?

A restaurant management app runs on phones and tablets, so waiters can take orders at the table and a kitchen screen can be a tablet on the wall. A desktop system suits a fixed counter or back office. Many restaurants use both, which makes it worth checking that one account works across every device you plan to use.

What does restaurant management software cost?

Compare the total cost for your actual setup, rather than the advertised starting price. Ask whether a quote is per location, per account or per device, and whether kitchen screens and reporting cost extra.

  • Subscription: the monthly charge and any required add-ons.
  • Hardware: phones, tablets, stands, printers and replacements.
  • Payments: any separate card terminal, provider contract or processing charges.
  • Setup: menu entry, staff training and transferring existing data.
  • Connectivity: internet access for live synchronization between devices.

A five-step test before you switch

  1. Create a small sample menu. Add a few of your real items and prices.
  2. Run a complete order. Include a customer note, a quantity change and checkout.
  3. Connect the kitchen. Confirm that another device receives the correct items.
  4. Check the numbers. Compare the receipt, sales report and inventory change.
  5. Test your environment. Check printer compatibility and what happens when your connection drops. Live synchronization requires connectivity.
